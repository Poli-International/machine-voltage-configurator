Machine Voltage Configurator Documentation

Professional voltage and speed calibration tool for rotary and coil tattoo machines

Overview

The Machine Voltage Configurator gives a tattoo artist a starting voltage to dial in from, based on machine type, stroke length and technique. It is a starting point, not a calibration: machines, motors, springs, grips and power supplies vary enough that the right setting is the one you find on your own machine, on skin, by ear and by feel. Read the number as "begin about here", then adjust.

Key Features

  • Dynamic Voltage Dial - Interactive SVG-based visualization of power output with 0.1V adjustment increments
  • Machine Type Mapping - Calibration for slider and pen-style rotary motors, plus traditional coil profiles

Technical Details

The arithmetic is deliberately simple, and it is worth being plain about what it is and is not. It adds three hand-set numbers together:

voltage = baseVoltage + strokeModifier[length] + techniqueModifier[technique]

speedHz = voltage × hertzConstant[machineType]

impactForce = "Soft" below 6.5V, "Hard" above 8.5V, "Medium" between

These constants are not measured values and they are not taken from a published source. They are a plausible starting grid, nothing more. The volts-to-hertz figure in particular is a rough proportion: on a rotary the motor speed does broadly follow the voltage, but on a coil machine the frequency is set mostly by the spring tension, the contact screw gap and the armature bar, so the same voltage on two coil machines will not give the same hertz. Do not quote these numbers as specifications, and do not use them in place of your machine manufacturer’s own guidance.

Frequently Asked Questions

What voltage is safe for my motor?

Always check your machine's manual. Most modern rotaries are designed for a 4V to 12V operating range.

How do I convert Volts to Hertz?

Use our configurator to input your machine's 'Motor RPM per Volt' to see the exact Hertz (hits per second).

Does higher voltage mean more depth?

No. Voltage controls speed. Depth is controlled by your machine's 'Hang' and your physical hand pressure.

Why does my machine slow down on skin?

This is 'Drag.' You may need to increase voltage slightly or use a machine with higher 'Torque.'

Is wireless power less consistent?

High-end battery packs are extremely stable and match traditional power supplies in consistency.
